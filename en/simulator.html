Like at funding firms (prop firms): you pass if equity reaches the target, and you fail if at the worst point of any hour equity drops below the daily limit (calculated from the balance at the start of the day, 17:00 New York) or below the total limit (calculated from the starting balance). Real rules differ from one firm to another; always read them first.

          How to backtest properly

          1. Write your rules before you start. When you enter, where you put the stop loss and take profit, what risk you use and when you don't trade. Fill in the "Strategy" field and a short checklist; if you can't write the rule down, you can't test it.
          2. Collect at least 30-50 trades with the same rules, ideally across several sessions and periods (trend, range, high volatility). A few winning trades in a row prove nothing.
          3. No lookahead. Use "Random period" so the date stays hidden, and don't skip ahead to "see what happens next". Decide only with what's on the chart at the current bar; the simulator won't let you go back after entering.
          4. Log every trade. Add the setup and a short note (why you entered), including on the losing ones. Then send the session to your journal.
          5. Judge by expectancy and drawdown, not by win rate. A strategy with 40% winners can be profitable at 1:2. Compare strategies in the table above and change only one rule at a time.
          6. Then on demo. A good backtest is a starting point: confirm the strategy for a few weeks on a demo account before using real money.

          How the simulator calculates

          • The chart shows the Bid price. A Buy enters at the Ask (Bid plus a fixed spread) and exits at the Bid; a Sell enters at the Bid and exits at the Ask. Fixed spread: EUR/USD 1.0 pip, GBP/USD 1.5, USD/JPY 1.2, AUD/USD 1.2, GBP/JPY 2.5, gold 3.0 pips (0.30 USD). No commission or swap.
          • On every new bar we check the high and the low. If both the stop loss and the take profit are hit in the same bar, we assume the stop loss was hit first (the conservative option), because the candle doesn't show the order.
          • If the bar opens beyond the stop loss (a gap), the exit is at the open price, as in reality. A limit or stop order filled in a bar can be stopped out in that same bar, but the take profit is only checked from the next bar onwards.
          • The lot is calculated as in the lot size calculator (rounded down to 0.01), from the current balance and the risk %. Pip: 0.0001; 0.01 on JPY pairs; 0.10 on gold. To convert into the account currency we use the historical closing rate for that day.
          • Results are in R: 1R is the amount risked at entry. That way you can see whether the strategy makes sense, whatever the account size.

          Important: past results, including those obtained in the simulator, don't guarantee future results. The simulator is an educational tool with virtual money, not a signal or a trading recommendation. Write your conclusions down in your journal and then practise on a demo account.

          Historical data: HistData.com (1-minute candles, which we aggregate into H1, H4 and D1; the trading day starts at 17:00 New York time; the data is updated monthly). From February to July 2023 the source has many missing hours, so that period isn't used in sessions.           How to use the simulator

          1. Press "Start session". Defaults: EUR/USD, H1, random period (the date stays hidden) and 1% risk. The chart opens full screen; the arrow at the top left takes you back to the settings.
          2. Move forward bar by bar with ▶| (or the → key), with +10 or with auto-play ▶ (the Space key). Future bars aren't visible and you can't go back.
          3. Place your order: choose Sell or Buy and press the big button (or the S and B keys). The stop loss and take profit (1:2) are placed automatically; drag the lines on the chart to where you want them. The lot size is calculated from your balance and risk %.
          4. Manage the position: on every bar we check the SL and TP. You can move the stop loss to break-even or close manually.
          5. At the end you find out the real period and see the statistics, the equity curve and the list of trades. You can export them to CSV or send them to your journal.
