Economic news

Economic calendar

The week's key economic news in one place, shown in your local time. I check it before I open any trade.

The economic calendar shows you when the data and decisions that can move currencies sharply come out: the US jobs report, inflation, central bank decisions and much more. For each release you see the currency affected, your local time, how long until the announcement, how important it is and the expected figures.

As a beginner, you don't need to trade the news. You just need to know when it's coming, so a sudden move doesn't catch you with an open position or a stop loss that's too tight. That's why I check the calendar every Sunday for the week ahead and every morning for the day.

Quick guide

The news that moves the market

You don't need to know them all. These are the ones I follow every month. Times are given in the local time of the market that publishes them; the live calendar above converts everything to your time.

NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls)

High impact

The US jobs report: how many new jobs were created outside agriculture, plus the unemployment rate and wage growth.

WhenUsually on the first Friday of the month, at 8:30 New York time, which is 12:30 UTC for most of the year.

CPI (inflation)

High impact

The consumer price index shows how fast prices are rising. It matters a lot, because central banks set interest rates based on inflation.

WhenMonthly, for every major economy. In the US, usually around the middle of the month, at 8:30 New York time (12:30 UTC for most of the year).

Interest rate decisions

High impact

The Fed (US), the ECB (euro area), the BoE (UK) and the BoJ (Japan) set their benchmark rate 8 times a year each. The press conference afterwards matters too: a different tone than expected can move a currency as much as the decision itself.

WhenFed: decision at 14:00 New York time, press conference at 14:30. ECB: decision at 14:15 Frankfurt time, press conference at 14:45. BoE: 12:00 London time. BoJ: no fixed time, usually during the Tokyo morning (local times of each central bank).

FOMC minutes

Medium impact

The detailed summary of the Fed's last meeting. The market searches it for clues about the next rate decisions.

WhenThree weeks after each Fed decision, at 14:00 New York time (18:00 UTC for most of the year).

PIB (GDP)

Medium to high impact

Gross domestic product shows how much the economy has grown or shrunk. It's published quarterly, often in several successive estimates.

WhenQuarterly. In the US, the first estimate comes out at 8:30 New York time (12:30 UTC for most of the year).

PMI

Medium impact

Surveys of purchasing managers in manufacturing and services. Above 50 means expansion, below 50 means contraction. They're among the earliest signals of how the economy is doing.

WhenMonthly, for the US, the euro area, Germany, the UK and other economies.

Retail sales

Medium impact

How much consumers spent in stores and online. Consumer spending is a very large part of the US economy.

WhenMonthly. In the US, usually around the middle of the month, at 8:30 New York time (12:30 UTC for most of the year).

Jobless claims

Medium impact

How many people in the US filed for unemployment benefits for the first time in the previous week (Initial Jobless Claims). A quick thermometer for the labour market.

WhenWeekly, usually on Thursday, at 8:30 New York time (12:30 UTC for most of the year).

The US and Europe change their clocks on different dates. For a few weeks in March and from late October, the time gap between New York and Europe is one hour shorter than usual, so US news arrives an hour earlier for European traders. The calendar above converts everything to your time automatically, and on the Sessions page you can see live which markets are open.

How important is a release

  • Low

    Usually has a small effect on price.

  • Medium

    Can move the pairs tied to that currency, especially if the figure surprises.

  • High

    NFP, inflation, rate decisions. Sudden moves, wide spreads and slippage.

By default, the calendar on this page shows high and medium impact news. You can also see low impact news by choosing "All".

Actual, forecast, previous

Actual
The published figure. It only appears at the moment of the announcement.
Forecast (consensus)
What the market expects: the average of the estimates made before the announcement.
Previous (prior)
The figure from the previous period, sometimes revised.

In the calendar above you see the forecast and the previous value. You'll find the actual figure on ForexFactory or TradingView right after the announcement.

Price reacts to the surprise, meaning the difference between the actual figure and the forecast, not to the figure itself. A good but already expected number may barely move the price, while an unexpected one can trigger a big move, in either direction. Nobody knows the direction in advance, so don't try to guess it.

Risk management

How to protect yourself

The rules I follow around the news. They are risk management rules, not trading recommendations.

  1. 01

    Check the week on Sunday and the day each morning

    On Sunday I look at which big releases are coming in the week ahead, and each morning I check that day. Write down the times of the high-impact news for the pairs you follow.

  2. 02

    No new trades 15–30 minutes before and after

    Around a high-impact release, I don't open new positions. I let the market calm down and only then look at the chart again.

  3. 03

    Spreads widen and slippage appears

    Just before and right after the announcement, liquidity drops. The spread can widen several times over, and orders can fill at a different price than the one you asked for.

  4. 04

    Your stop loss can be filled at a worse price

    If the price jumps past your level, your stop loss closes at the first available price. The loss can be bigger than you planned, even if you did everything right.

  5. 05

    Reduce your lot or stay out

    If you do trade on a day with big news, use a smaller risk and recalculate your lot in the calculator. Not trading at all is also a good decision.

  6. 06

    Swing trading: watch the positions you hold through the news

    If you hold a position for several days, check which big releases are coming before your target. Decide in advance, in your plan, whether you keep it, reduce your risk or close it.

Remember

  • You check the calendar before every trading day and know the times of the big releases.
  • High-impact news brings wider spreads, slippage and sudden moves.
  • Price reacts to the surprise versus the forecast, not to the figure itself.
  • No new trades right before and after big news. When in doubt, stay out.
