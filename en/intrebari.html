Frequently asked questions

The questions I get asked most often, with straight answers. No promises of profit.

How much money do I need to start?

None. You start on a demo account, with virtual money, and stay there until you're disciplined: at least 6 to 8 weeks and 30 to 50 trades, with the same plan and the same risk you'd use live.

Only then do you move to a small live account, with an amount you can afford to lose entirely without it affecting your life. No borrowed money and no rent money. With 0.01 lots and 1% risk per trade, a small account is enough to learn.

How long does it take to become profitable?

I can't promise you that, and nobody can. I taught myself, and it took me many years of mistakes, patience and discipline to start trading consistently. Many people never get there, and it's good to know that from the start.

What you can control is the process: small risk, a written plan, a journal and patience. Results come, if they come, after a great many trades, not after a few weeks.

With the beginner course: 8 chapters, from pips and lots to your first trading plan. When you've finished it, take the 15-question quiz to see what you've understood, then practise lot sizing with the calculator.

Whichever broker you choose, check first which entity opens your account and who regulates it, what the costs are and how easily you can withdraw your money. A broker outside the EU isn't required to apply the protections for EU retail clients, such as capped leverage and negative balance protection.

Is Forex trading legal where I live?

In most countries, trading Forex and CFDs is legal as long as you do it through a broker authorised by a financial regulator. The rules depend on your country, so check your local regulator's website: many regulators publish lists of authorised firms and warnings about unauthorised ones.

In the EU, the ESMA restrictions on CFDs apply to retail clients: capped leverage (1:30 on the major pairs), positions closed out at 50% of the required margin, negative balance protection and risk warnings. Outside the EU, protections vary from country to country. Always check the broker before you deposit any money.

What taxes do I pay on my trading profits?

In most countries, trading profits are taxable, but the rules depend on where you live. In general terms:

  • In some countries, if you use a local broker, tax may be withheld automatically on your gains.
  • With a broker based abroad, you often have to declare your net gains yourself in your annual tax return and pay the tax.
  • Depending on your income and your country, other contributions (for example social or health contributions) may also apply.

Tax rules change often, and I'm not a tax adviser. Check the current rules with your local tax authority or ask an accountant.

Can I trade from my phone?

Yes. MetaTrader 4 and MetaTrader 5 have phone apps where you can open, monitor and close positions. For analysis, though, I recommend a computer: on a big screen you see the chart more clearly and you're less likely to get a level or the lot size wrong. Before any order you place from your phone, double-check the lot size and the stop loss.

What is a demo account?

An account with virtual money and real prices, offered for free by brokers. You learn the platform and test your plan without any risk. Treat it like a live account: the same 1% risk, the same plan, a complete journal.

Keep two differences in mind: on demo you don't feel the same emotions, and execution can be slightly different from a live account. That's why, when you go live, you start with small amounts.

Why do most people who trade lose money?

Brokers regulated in the EU must show what percentage of retail accounts lose money on CFDs, and ESMA's analyses have shown that most retail accounts lose. The typical reasons: too much risk per trade, maximum leverage, no stop loss, no written plan and emotional decisions.

There's no guarantee you'll be in the minority. What you can do is eliminate the avoidable mistakes: you'll find them one by one in the course and the basic rules.

Which timeframe should I use?

I'm a swing trader and work mainly on D1 and H4. To start with, I recommend the higher timeframes: they have less noise, fewer false signals and give you time to think. You start on D1 to see the direction and drop down to H4 to look for the entry. Timeframes of a few minutes are the hardest for a beginner.

How much time does it take each day?

With swing trading you don't have to sit in front of the charts all day. On D1 and H4 you do your analysis a few times a day, after the candles close, and positions stay open for days or weeks, with stop loss and take profit set.

At the beginning, put more time into learning than into trading: the course, the demo account, the journal. Reviewing your journal every weekend matters more than the hours you spend in front of the screen each day.
