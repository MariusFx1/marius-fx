Shapes on the chart

Chart patterns

Ten chart patterns and three candlestick patterns common in forex education. Each drawing shows the shape and a teaching plan: where you enter, where you place the stop loss and where you take profit.

The patterns below are schematic examples, not trading signals.

  • Entry
  • Stop loss (SL)
  • Take profit (TP)
  • H = pattern height (measured move)

The levels in the drawings are for teaching. On a real chart you adjust them to structure, spread and volatility, and you risk only a small percentage of your account per trade. Calculate the lot size.

You only enter if the take profit gives you at least 2R (minimum risk/reward ratio of 1:2).

Chart patterns

01

Head and shoulders

Head and Shoulders usually appears after a rise. The price makes a first peak, the left shoulder, drops, climbs higher to form the head, then drops again and makes a third peak, the right shoulder, lower than the head. The lows between the peaks can be joined into a neckline. On the chart you look for the three peaks and watch whether the price breaks below the neckline; the shape alone doesn't tell you what comes next.

Trade plan

  • Entry: sell after a candle closes below the neckline (or on its retest).
  • Stop loss: above the right shoulder.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Entering before the neckline breaks: the pattern isn’t complete yet.
  • Seeing “head and shoulders” in any three peaks, without a clear rise before them.
02

Inverse head and shoulders

The mirror version of head and shoulders usually appears after a decline: a low (left shoulder), a deeper low (head) and a shallower low (right shoulder). The peaks between them form the neckline. The shape only becomes relevant once the price closes above that line.

Trade plan

  • Entry: buy after a close above the neckline (or on its retest).
  • Stop loss: below the right shoulder.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Placing the stop loss just below the neckline, where a simple retest can hit it.
03

Double top

A Double Top forms when the price rises twice to roughly the same level and doesn't clearly break above it. Between the two peaks there's a low, and the neckline is drawn through that low. The outline looks like the letter "M". A drop below the neckline is what you note as a possible breakout, not as a certainty.

Trade plan

  • Entry: sell after a close below the neckline (the low between the peaks).
  • Stop loss: above the two peaks.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Selling at the second peak, before the breakout: the price often keeps going up.
04

Double bottom

A Double Bottom is the reverse shape and often appears after a decline. The price touches a similar low zone twice, with a peak between them. The neckline runs through that peak. You watch whether the price rises and breaks above the line. It's a pattern to recognise on the chart, not an invitation to enter a trade.

Trade plan

  • Entry: buy after a close above the neckline (the peak between the lows).
  • Stop loss: below the two lows.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Calling two lows at very different levels a “double bottom”.
05

Triple top / triple bottom

It looks like a double top, but the price tests the same resistance three times without getting through, and the lows between the peaks form a support. The triple bottom is the mirror image: three touches of the same support, then a break above the resistance between them, with the plan reversed (buy, stop loss below the lows).

Trade plan

  • Entry: sell after a close below the support formed by the lows.
  • Stop loss: above the three peaks (or above the last peak, if it is lower).
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Mistaking an ongoing range (rectangle) for a triple top: without a break of support there is no pattern.
06

Flag

A Flag starts with a short, steep move, called the impulse or the pole. After it, the price settles into a small channel, usually sloping against the impulse: that's the flag, with two almost parallel edges. In the drawing, the breakout leaves the channel in the direction of the impulse. The channel may not be confirmed; the picture only shows the shape.

Trade plan

  • Entry: in the direction of the impulse, after a close outside the channel.
  • Stop loss: beyond the opposite end of the flag (below the last low, for a bullish flag).
  • Take profit: the pole length (H), projected from the breakout point, but only if it offers at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • The pullback is too deep (it retraces most of the pole): it is no longer a flag.
  • Buying inside the channel, before the breakout.
07

Wedge

A wedge has two lines sloping in the same direction that converge. The rising wedge climbs with more and more difficulty: the lows rise faster than the peaks, and the classic breakout is down. The falling wedge is its mirror image: it falls more and more slowly and you watch for a breakout up.

Trade plan

  • Entry: sell after a close below the lower line (for a falling wedge: buy above the upper line).
  • Stop loss: above the last peak inside the wedge.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing the wedge with a parallel channel: in a wedge the lines must clearly converge.
  • Rushing to sell just because the rise “looks tired”, without a breakout.
08

Triangle

A symmetrical Triangle is drawn when the highs keep getting lower and the lows keep getting higher, until the two lines converge. The price moves in an ever narrower space. You mark the upper line as resistance and the lower line as support and watch where the price breaks out. There are also ascending and descending triangles; none of them tells you the direction on its own.

Trade plan

  • Entry: in the direction of the breakout, after a close outside the triangle.
  • Stop loss: beyond the last peak or low inside the triangle.
  • Take profit: the widest part of the triangle (H), projected from the breakout point, but only if it offers at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Guessing the direction before the breakout.
  • Entering on a breakout very close to the apex, where the shape has usually lost its meaning.
09

Rectangle (range)

In a rectangle the price oscillates between an almost horizontal support and resistance, with no clear direction. You can treat the zone in two ways: wait for one edge to break or, while the range holds, look for reactions at the edges. Both options need a stop loss beyond the edge you use.

Trade plan

  • Entry: after a close above resistance (buy) or below support (sell).
  • Stop loss: back inside the range, usually around its middle.
  • Take profit: the measured move H extended (towards 1.5–2×H or to the next structure level), so that the target is at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • Entering on the first poke through the edge, which turns out to be just a false breakout (fakeout).
  • Trading the middle of the range, where risk and target are poorly placed.
10

Cup & Handle

Cup and handle usually appears in an uptrend: the price falls smoothly and returns in a “U” shape to the starting level, then makes a small pullback, the handle. The cup should be rounded, not a “V”, and the handle should be small compared with the cup.

Trade plan

  • Entry: buy after a close above the cup rim (the top of the handle).
  • Stop loss: below the low of the handle.
  • Take profit: the cup depth H, projected up from its rim, but only if it offers at least 2R.

Common mistakes

  • The handle drops too deep (close to the middle of the cup): the shape loses its meaning.

Candlestick patterns

Signals made of one or two candles. They only matter at an important level (support, resistance, trendline), not anywhere on the chart.

11

Engulfing

One candle fully “swallows” the body of the previous candle, in the opposite direction. A bullish engulfing (green after red) matters mostly at support, after a decline; a bearish one (red after green) at resistance, after a rise. It has no “measured” target like the larger patterns, so the target is set by structure and the risk/reward ratio.

Trade plan

  • Entry: buy above the high of the engulfing candle (or at its close).
  • Stop loss: below the low of the engulfing candle.
  • Take profit: at the next resistance, but only if it offers at least 2R (twice the distance to the stop loss).

Common mistakes

  • Taking any engulfing in the middle of the chart, far from support or resistance.
  • The candle is so large that the stop loss no longer fits your risk per trade.
12

Pin bar

A pin bar has a small body and a long wick (at least two to three times the body) showing that the price was rejected from a zone. A long lower wick at support suggests sellers were rejected; a long upper wick at resistance, that buyers were rejected.

Trade plan

  • Entry: buy above the pin bar high (or on a 50% pullback into the wick).
  • Stop loss: a few pips below the tip of the wick.
  • Take profit: at the next resistance, but only if it offers at least 2R (twice the distance to the stop loss).

Common mistakes

  • Trading a pin bar against a strong trend, with no level behind it.
13

Inside bar

An inside bar is a candle whose high and low sit inside the previous candle (the mother candle). It shows a pause, a compression before a move. It is most often used in the direction of the trend, after an impulse.

Trade plan

  • Entry: on the break of the mother candle high (or its low, in a downtrend).
  • Stop loss: below the mother candle low (tighter option: at its midpoint).
  • Take profit: at the next resistance, but only if it offers at least 2R (twice the distance to the stop loss).

Common mistakes

  • Taking every inside bar in a sideways market, where false breakouts are frequent.

Quiz: which pattern is this?

Pick the answer for each drawing. You find out right away if you got it.

  1. 1/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! Two peaks at the same level, then a break below the low between them.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Double top. Two peaks at the same level, then a break below the low between them.

  2. 2/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! Three lows, the middle one the deepest, then a break above the neckline.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Inverse head and shoulders. Three lows, the middle one the deepest, then a break above the neckline.

  3. 3/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! A steep impulse (pole), then a small channel sloping against it and continuation.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Flag. A steep impulse (pole), then a small channel sloping against it and continuation.

  4. 4/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! Both lines rise and converge: a rising wedge, broken to the downside.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Wedge. Both lines rise and converge: a rising wedge, broken to the downside.

  5. 5/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! A rounded “U” cup, a small handle, then a break above the rim.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Cup & Handle. A rounded “U” cup, a small handle, then a break above the rim.

  6. 6/6

    Which pattern is this?

    Correct! Small body and a long lower wick at support: rejection.

    Not quite. The correct answer: Pin bar. Small body and a long lower wick at support: rejection.
